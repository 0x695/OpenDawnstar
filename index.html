The Assignment

Restore Your Good Name

Your posting to Dawnstar begins badly. Glacier tribes are raiding the far northern town, and Governor Cyril Vinticae has a problem he can't solve alone.

He needs you to find his four champions — Alhavara, Beatrice, Chung, and Delacroix — because one of them is almost certainly working with the tribes. You'll need to survive the dungeons ringing the town, choose your allies carefully, and gather enough power to face down whoever's been feeding the raiders their information.

Unlike most Elder Scrolls games, Dawnstar has exactly one quest. No side content, no distractions — just the raid, the four suspects, and the clock.

“Creatures and foes pressed around you but you emerged victorious. Weary, you seek Governor Vinticae to inform him Dawnstar is once again his.”

— from the original game's victory text

The World

One Camp. Four Directions. Thirty-Six Dungeons.

Dawnstar itself — camp, to the locals — sits at the center of everything. Four gates lead out in the four cardinal directions; each one opens onto a chain of nine connected dungeons, three zones of three, before the trail runs cold.

Camp is also the only truly safe ground — home to the four peddlers, the healer Eustacia, and, once the traitor is revealed, the site of the final battle: every enemy in the game converging at once, capped by the strongest monster in Dawnstar, the Gehenoth Thriceborn.

About This Port

Rebuilt, Not Emulated

This isn't the original game running in an emulator — it's a ground-up rewrite in plain JavaScript, built by reverse-engineering the original 2003 .jar's binary data files byte by byte, then cross-checking every formula against an independent Python decompilation and UESP's own wiki pages.

Combat, dungeon generation, character progression, shops, spells, and the champion questline all use the real formulas traced from the original source — not approximations dressed up to look right. Where something genuinely couldn't be traced, it's documented as a substitution rather than passed off as authentic.

OpenDawnstar is a fan project. The Elder Scrolls Travels: Dawnstar was originally developed by Vir2L Studios and published by Bethesda Softworks / ZeniMax Media in 2003. OpenDawnstar is an unofficial, non-commercial, reverse-engineered reimplementation: all game logic is original code, written from scratch by reading the original's data files and cross-referencing an independent decompilation — no original code or bytecode is included. The original game's own extracted sprite and audio-adjacent assets are used for visual fidelity. OpenDawnstar is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by ZeniMax Media Inc.
